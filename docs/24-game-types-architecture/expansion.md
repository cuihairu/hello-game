# Chapter Expansion Plan: Game Development Knowledge System Expansion Roadmap

> This site's home turf has always been the game backend — real-time-ness, consistency, concurrency and state are the parts where this site has the most accumulated depth, and that positioning does not change. But game development is a whole: people who build backends cannot avoid client-side constraints, and people who build clients need to understand the server's adjudication. The quality of collaboration between design, testing, operations and engineering directly caps the product ceiling. This page is the roadmap for the site's expansion from a "game knowledge system" into a complete game development knowledge system: it inventories current coverage and lays out the backfill plan and rollout cadence.

> **Status (2026-09)**: the first round of expansion is fully landed — all three of 19/20/21 are in the sidebar "extension" group, and both cross-cutting entries (history line and games library) are in the navigation. This page is kept as the record of the expansion process and the registration entry point for follow-up increments.

---

## 1. Why Expand

Two practical reasons:

1. **Backend knowledge cannot stand alone.** Lecture 03 covers frontend engines, because client-side platform constraints push back and determine the server-side design space; Lecture 12 covers data analytics, because server transaction records are the factual basis of analysis. By the same logic, the rendering pipeline, resource loading and UI frameworks — these "frontend things" — hold a large number of problems that need to be decided jointly by frontend and backend (response size, loading strategy, prediction and correction).
2. **Collaboration is part of the knowledge system.** How the art pipeline ships assets, how QA validates a build, how audio enters the project — backend engineers do not need to be able to do these, but they need to know where the interfaces are, where the cost lies, and when to call a stop.

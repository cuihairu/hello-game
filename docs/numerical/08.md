# Numerical Reading List and References

## Category

## Definition


This page collects classic books and public references on numerical and game design, each annotated with **the gap it fills and its core message**. Inclusion discipline: only entries with verifiable sources are collected (title, author, publisher, and year stated as-is); entries with no reliable source are not collected. The backend-oriented reading list is separate — see the README reference books and [the reference bibliography of Feature Note 15](/24-game-types-architecture/15-references); this page is complementary and non-redundant with them.

## Problem

Numerical references most easily fall into two traps: collecting secondhand retellings with no traceable source, or a reading list that does not match the actual gaps. Inclusion discipline (verifiable sources, annotated gaps) makes the list a usable reading trail rather than a bookmark folder.


### Design Master Plans

**The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses**
- **Author**: Jesse Schell; first edition 2008, CRC Press; Chinese edition available
- **Gap it fills**: the master plan for design thinking — the first 19 chapters of this site cover "how to build", and this book covers "why build it this way"
- **Core message**: dissects experience, mechanics, and player psychology through a "lenses" methodology, turning "fun" into a checklist of questions that can be asked and verified; numerical designers use it to establish the working order of "experience goals before numbers" (the origin of the conventions on page 01 of this chapter)

**Level Up! The Guide to Great Video Game Design**
- **Author**: Scott Rogers; first edition 2010, Wiley; Chinese edition available
- **Gap it fills**: level structure and pacing — the level-design perspective on the difficulty curve on page 07 of this chapter
- **Core message**: from level layout and pacing control to design document writing, richly illustrated; the most direct paradigm for "how to structure the new player's first 30 minutes"

**Rules of Play: Game Design Fundamentals**
- **Authors**: Katie Salen, Eric Zimmerman; MIT Press, 2003
- **Gap it fills**: the theoretical foundation of design — a formal framework for rules, play, and meaning
- **Core message**: divides "rules" into three layers — formal rules, experience rules, and cultural rules; the theoretical reference for systematic discussion of the relationship between mechanics and numbers (academic-leaning, recommended as a tool book for lookup)

### Numerical and Balance

**Game Balance**
- **Authors**: Brenda Romero, Ian Schreiber; CRC Press, 2021
- **Gap it fills**: a dedicated treatise on numerical balance — the only systematic book source on balance on this site besides Feature Note 02 (implementation side)
- **Core message**: randomness, trade and market economics, progression systems, PVP balance, cost-return curves, with recomputable models given topic by topic — corresponding directly to the design-side unfolding of pages 02-05 of this chapter

**Game Design Workshop**
- **Author**: Tracy Fullerton; first edition 2004 (fourth edition available as of 2018), CRC Press; Chinese edition available
- **Gap it fills**: hands-on methods for mechanics and prototypes — putting numbers into a playable prototype for verification
- **Core message**: dissects gameplay using the "mechanics-dynamics-aesthetics" framework, emphasizing paper prototypes and playtest iteration; the verification closed loop of numerical design (page 01 of this chapter) has a complete workflow demonstration here

**Designing Games: A Guide to Engineering Experiences**
- **Author**: Tynan Sylvester; O'Reilly, 2013; Chinese edition available
- **Gap it fills**: decision depth and emotional design — why some numerical systems are "replayable"
- **Core message**: analyzes game depth through decision theory (variance, information, discounting), explaining how randomness creates emotional arcs — one of the methodological origins of "expectation determines value-for-money, variance determines emotion" on page 05 of this chapter

### Public References

- **Game Balance Concepts (free course)**: Ian Schreiber's ten-week open course on numerical balance (published 2010, searchable by course name), the predecessor of *Game Balance* — the best numerical introduction among free materials, with weekly exercises
- **Edward Castronova, Virtual Worlds: A First-Hand Account of Market and Society on the Cyberian Frontier (2001, working paper)**: an early representative work of virtual economy research, one of the academic origins of the faucet/drain production-consumption model (page 04 of this chapter)
- **GDC Talk Library (GDC Vault free section)**: search by game balance / economy design; post-mortem talks by front-line numerical designers — free and continuously updated
- **Cross-links within this site**: all implementation-side companions are in [Feature Note 02 "Numerical and Economy Systems"]( /24-game-types-architecture/02-game-design); data conventions are in [Feature Note 12](/24-game-types-architecture/12-data-analytics) and [Chapter 14](/operation/bi/); the economy accounting boundary is on the Chapter 13 [Transactions and Payments page](/system/services/03)

### Cross-References by Topic

Game Balance Concepts focuses on numerical balance calculation, while *The Art of Game Design* focuses on the experience perspective; *Game Balance* can be cross-checked with the models on pages 02–05 of this site, *Game Design Workshop* discusses prototype verification, and *Rules of Play* and *Designing Games* cover mechanics and decision depth. Whether a model in a project holds up ultimately still requires recomputation with the project's own data.

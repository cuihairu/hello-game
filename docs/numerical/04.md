# Economy Loop and Faucet and Sink

## Category

Numerical tree. Node positioning: the structure of the economy loop — draw the faucet/drain diagram first, currency tiering, four mature forms of drain, inflation monitoring and adjustment.

## Definition


A game economy is an artificial system: all currency and resources are produced by the system (faucet, the tap) and reclaimed by the system (drain, the sewer). Only two outcomes exist for a faucet-sink imbalance — inflation (currency devalues, payment and grind both lose meaning) and deflation (too little is produced, players check out). This page covers the loop structure, currency tiering and reclamation means from the design side; the ledger and accounting on the implementation side are in Section 6 of [Feature Note 02](/24-game-types-architecture/02-game-design) and [Chapter 13, page 03 "An Economy System Is Not Inventory Arithmetic"]( /system/services/03).

## Problem

Only two outcomes exist for a faucet-sink imbalance: inflation (currency devalues, payment and grind both lose meaning) and deflation (players check out). If you populate numbers without first drawing the loop diagram, inflation only surfaces months after launch, by which point reclamation means are hard to insert into players' existing expectations.


### The Loop Model: Draw the Diagram First, Then Populate Numbers

The first deliverable of any economy design is a **faucet-sink loop diagram**: for each gameplay, mark what it produces (currency, materials, equipment) and what it consumes (stamina, tickets, durability); for each reclamation port, mark what it reclaims and how much. The diagram's acceptance standard is **every currency has at least one production chain and two or more reclamation chains** — a single chain means that currency's flow is completely predictable, leaving the economy no room for adjustment.

Run the faucet-sink reconciliation as a table over one cycle: daily output − daily consumption = daily net increase. Example: a server produces 1M gold daily, reclamation ports digest only 400k, daily net increase 600k; at a stock of 30M, **the stock doubles every 50 days** — unless trading-house prices absorb correspondingly, devaluation is visible to the naked eye. Run this table separately by gameplay and by player tier (whale/medium/small spender, active/lightly active), because the faucet-sink structure differs completely across tiers.

### Currency Tiering

The common practice is a three-layer pyramid (consistent with Section 6.1 of Feature Note 02): **premium currency** (obtained by top-up, most universal), **game currency** (produced by gameplay, main consumption scenes), and **bound/event currency** (directed production, directed consumption). Three design disciplines: exchange between tiers is **one-way downward** (premium currency can convert to game currency, the reverse is forbidden — prevents studio arbitrage and collapse of payment willingness), each currency has a clear primary production and primary consumption scene ("universal currency" equals no currency at all), and keep the variety restrained (3-4 is ideal, more and players cannot keep score, fewer and design loses flexibility).

### Reclamation Design: Four Mature Forms of Drain

- **Hard consumption**: repair fees, teleport fees, trade taxes — charged per action, large volume and stable, the economy's bedrock
- **Progression consumption**: enhancement, leveling, crafting — converts currency into combat power, highest player acceptance, but must align with the growth curve (page 03 of this chapter), otherwise reclamation becomes a "penalty"
- **Appearance and status consumption**: outfits, titles, mounts — no bearing on combat-power balance, pure willingness payment, the best relief valve during inflation
- **Probabilistic consumption**: gacha, chests — expectation is controllable (see page 05 of this chapter), variance brings thrill, reclamation efficiency is highest but also most sensitive, and must be bound by probability disclosure and compliance (see Chapter 17)

One anti-pattern in reclamation design to watch for: **using a premium-only reclamation port to solve server-wide inflation** — inflation is cross-tier, so opening a relief valve only for whales while medium and small spenders' currency keeps devaluing.

### Inflation Monitoring and Adjustment

Early signals (aligned with the conventions in Section 6.4 of Feature Note 02): week-over-week change in the trading-house core price index, share of large-denomination currency hoarding (top players stockpiling coins), falling currency velocity. Adjustment means, from low to high intrusiveness: add reclamation ports (new progression lines, new appearances), adjust output (event pacing, stamina pricing), structural reclamation (binding, expiry). **Adjustment should always choose between "adding on the output side" and "adding on the reclamation side", avoiding direct cuts to player stock** — directly devaluing players' historical accumulation is the most trust-damaging option.

### Common Pitfalls

- **Designing only production, not reclamation**: only after three months of launch do you start hunting for a "relief valve", by which point inflation is a fact
- **Betting all reclamation on progression**: once progression saturates, reclamation fails and inflation returns — reclamation chains must be diverse
- **The faucet-sink table computes only the mean**: mean balanced, tiers imbalanced (small spenders face inflation, whales face deflation) is the most common hidden failure
- **Reverse currency conversion**: premium currency can convert to value outside game currency, and an arbitrage chain appears overnight
- **Treating binding as a universal lock**: an overabundance of bound currency renders the trading system a hollow shell, and players lose the concept of "currency"

# Risk Control, Audit, GM and Customer Support

## Category

system / general services subtree. Node position: the four governance systems of risk control, audit, GM and support — risk control is more than detection, audit aims at accountability, GM is controlled manual intervention, support is hard in cross-system evidence collection, and the four must work together.

## Definition


These kinds of systems represent the governance side of a game project. Gameplay systems answer "what can the player do"; governance systems answer "what did the player do wrong, what did the system do wrong, and how does the team plan to handle it". Many projects only realize that governance is not adding a few buttons to the backend, but a complete system of evidence, disposition and coordination, once cheats, scams, wrongful bans, accidental grants and public sentiment all show up at the same time.

## Problem

If all four are built as isolated backends, the whole thing falls apart: risk control that only detects wrongly hurts the innocent and bans the wrong people, audit that only keeps logs cannot attribute responsibility, GM without approval and traces is a backdoor for insiders, and support that cannot collect evidence across systems can only pass the buck. Coordination is not icing on the cake; it is the precondition for each of them to hold up at all.


### Why risk control cannot stop at detection

Risk control is most easily understood as "write some rules to catch cheaters", but once it is really in production, detection alone is far from enough. A complete closed loop includes at least:

- Identification: logs, behavior features, device information, transaction paths, social propagation chains
- Adjudication: rule hits, model scores, manual review, whitelists and false-positive control
- Disposition: mutes, account bans, asset clawback, matchmaking pool quarantine, trade restrictions
- Tracking: whether the punishment took effect, whether it was bypassed, whether it recurred
- Appeal: after a player complains, can evidence be pulled, the path replayed and assets restored

If a system can only "slap on one cheat tag" but has no follow-up disposition and appeal path, then it is not really a governance capability yet.

### The goal of audit is not keeping logs, but being able to attribute responsibility

Many teams say "we have operation logs", but when something really goes wrong the logs do not answer the key questions. A qualified audit system must at least tell you:

- Who did what operation, at what time
- Which objects that operation changed, and what the values were before and after
- What the basis of the operation was, such as a ticket, an approval order, an event order, a script job
- Whether the operation actually executed successfully, and where it stopped when it failed

Scenarios where audit is especially important include:

- Support re-fulfillment and asset repair
- GM command execution
- Bulk reward issuance from the operations backend
- Bans and unbans
- Configuration hotfixes and script jobs

Recording only "someone clicked a button" is not enough; ideally you should also keep the pre-operation snapshot, the post-operation result and the scope of impact. Otherwise in an incident review you only know "the button was clicked", not what the actual damage was.

### The essence of a GM system is controlled manual intervention

The reason GM tooling is dangerous is not that it has many features, but that it naturally bypasses the normal business flow. It should be designed as "a controlled side channel that exists for firefighting and operational necessity", not as "a universal backdoor".

A mature GM system usually layers its operations:

- Read-only: query character, query order, query logs, query inventory
- Weak write: change nickname, send mail, adjust announcements
- Strong write: add or subtract assets, unlock progress, execute bans, migrate characters

Different layers need different permissions, approvals and audit intensity. Strong-write operations in particular should ideally have:

- Least-privilege principle
- Ticket number or reason required
- Two-person review or high-risk approval
- Rate limiting and bulk protection
- A revocable or compensable plan

Many classic incidents come from "temporarily grant a super admin to put out the fire", after which that permission is never taken back.

### What makes support hard is cross-system evidence collection

Support is superficially a ticket system, but in reality it is an aggregation query and limited disposition platform. When a user comes to complain, support rarely solves the problem by looking at one table; it usually needs to see at the same time:

- Login and character information
- Order and payment state
- Asset transaction records
- Chat and report records
- Punishment history
- Recent version updates, event reward issuance and abnormal announcements

This means the support system cannot be just a UI shell; it needs reliable data aggregation interfaces and permission boundaries. Otherwise front-line support either cannot find the information and can only escalate to R&D, or has permissions that are too broad and performs dangerous operations directly on production data.

### Why risk control, support and audit must coordinate

These three are often built separately by different teams, but in reality they share the same problem chain:

- Risk control is responsible for discovering suspicious behavior
- Audit is responsible for leaving trustworthy evidence
- Support is responsible for external communication and appeal handling
- GM or the operations backend is responsible for necessary manual disposition

If these systems are siloed, a bad situation appears:

- Risk control hit something, but support cannot explain why the ban happened
- Support promised compensation, but the backend has no precise way to roll the asset back
- Operations made a bulk operation mistake, but audit cannot confirm the affected list

What truly marks a mature governance capability is not how high the hit rate is, but whether after a dispute arises the team can produce evidence, carry out disposition and restore order within an acceptable time.

### Common mistakes

- Treating risk control as an independent algorithm project, not linking it to business rules, the punishment flow or support appeals
- Audit only records text logs, not the changed objects or before/after state
- GM permissions handed out too broadly for too long, with no approval, no take-back and no high-risk protection
- The support backend connects directly to the production database to query and modify data, creating a new risk source

The longer-running, more social and more commercialized a game project becomes, the closer governance systems come to core gameplay in importance. Because what truly sets a project's floor is usually not whether you can make a new event, but whether when cheats go rampant, assets go wrong or user complaints explode, you have a system that can hold the situation steady.

### Practical case: a risk-control false-positive investigation where "8,000 accounts were banned at 2 a.m., 40% reversed"

> The case is a demonstration example (not drawn from a real project), with numbers rounded and simplified. It takes this page's "Why risk control cannot stop at detection", "The goal of audit is not keeping logs, but being able to attribute responsibility", "The essence of a GM system is controlled manual intervention", "What makes support hard is cross-system evidence collection" and "Why risk control, support and audit must coordinate" into deep water: one bulk ban exposed four gaps at once — a broken closed loop, an approval bypass, missing snapshots, and split evidence collection.

**Background and symptoms**: A social project launched a new risk-control rule — "multiple accounts on the same IP + similar behavior", automatically banned when both conditions hit. The rule took effect at **02**:**00** in the early morning; the first night hit **8,000** accounts and all were executed automatically (strong-write operations went straight through the risk-control pipeline, no GM approval, no ticket number). At **09**:**00** the next day, appeal tickets flooded in at **1,200**, with player feedback concentrated on "five accounts on one internet café line" and "three accounts for one family of three"; front-line support only had the ticket shell, while IP attribution, device fingerprints and behavior records were scattered across three systems, so evidence collection could only be escalated to R&D. Public sentiment started to spread at midday.

### Step 1: Symptoms and caliber — first separate "how many were hit" from "how many should have been banned"

Caliber aligned: take the ban list as the full set, re-adjudicate by "IP attribution database + same-egress device count + behavior similarity weight", splitting into two categories — "real studios / false positives". First record two entries:

- **Hits**: **8,000** accounts, all automatically banned
- **Re-adjudicated**: real studios **4,600**, false positives **3,400** — false-positive rate **42.5**%

A hit is not the same as should-be-banned — settle the false-positive ledger first, then audit the rule and the pipeline ledgers.

### Step 2: Layered attribution — from the ban list to the governance pipeline ledger

```text
规则账   凌晨命中 8000：双条件全量自动封禁，共享出口（网吧/校园网/家庭多设备）无一豁免路径
复检账   30 小时联合复检（D1 14:00 → D2 20:00）：4600 + 3400 = 8000，误伤率 42.5%（3400/8000）
申诉账   申诉 1200 单仅为误伤的自发暴露；一线取证需跨三系统，升级研发排单 2 天起
改判账   解封 3400（48 小时分批完成）；误伤中充值用户 900，资产已被批量回收的 210 人逐个补偿
```

The ledger converges: the **42.5**% false-positive rate is not a rule-precision problem, it is a pipeline problem — the adjudication link lacks the "whitelists and false-positive control" this page talks about; the appeal link does not exist at all, and the **1,200** appeal tickets are only the part of the **3,400** false positives that surfaced on its own; being able to complete the re-adjudication depended on **30** hours of manually stitching data from three systems, not on any ready-made evidence pipeline.

### Step 3: Root cause — the closed loop only had three of five links built, and strong writes went through the bypass

Against this page's five-link closed loop in "Why risk control cannot stop at detection": identification, adjudication and disposition ran through overnight, but the tracking and appeal links were absent — "after a player complains, can evidence be pulled, the path replayed and assets restored" had no answer at all. Against "The essence of a GM system is controlled manual intervention": bulk banning belongs to the **strong write** class in this page's layering, and should have been paired with "two-person review or high-risk approval" plus "rate limiting and bulk protection", but the risk-control straight-through pipeline bypassed the whole thing — a variant of this page's "many classic incidents come from 'temporarily grant a super admin to put out the fire'": not permission over-issuance, but the strong-write capability never entered GM layering in the first place. Against "The goal of audit is not keeping logs, but being able to attribute responsibility": the logs only had the four words "ban action" — no adjudication-basis snapshot, no affected list, no before/after state — "recording only 'someone clicked a button' is not enough" came true word for word. Two of the siloed situations in the coordination section hit: "risk control hit something, but support cannot explain why the ban happened"; "support promised compensation, but the backend has no precise way to roll the asset back" — the **210**-person compensation list was dug out backwards from the ban pipeline by audit.

### Step 4: Disposition and backfill — reverse the rulings first, then close the loop back into a ring

The fix came in two parts. Emergency: at **D1 14**:**00** a three-party joint re-review started, and at **D2 20**:**00** the full re-adjudication was complete (**30** hours); the **3,400** false positives were unbanned in batches within **48** hours; the **210** people whose assets had been reclaimed were compensated one by one against the audit trail, and the **900** paying users received an apology and compensation mail; the public-sentiment announcement was aligned with the reversal caliber. Institutional, four steps: strong-write bulk banning was moved into GM layering — two-person review, with any single batch over **500** forcing high-risk approval; disposition actions keep a snapshot of the adjudication basis and the affected list, so audit can attribute responsibility down to "why banned, whom banned, on what basis"; appeal evidence packs are generated automatically (adjudication basis + behavior replay + restoration path), so appeals no longer escalate to R&D; the support aggregation query interface pulls all six data classes in one call, so front line never touches the production database. Recheck: the rule was relaunched the following month, hitting **5,200** with **250** false positives (**4.8**%), appeal tickets averaging **8** minutes to handle, and the approval block for batches over **500** triggering **2** times that month.

**Backfill checklist**:

| Landing point | Action | Backfill location |
|-----|------|--------|
| Approval bypass | Strong-write bulk banning moved into GM layering, two-person review + high-risk approval for any batch over 500 | The essence of a GM system is controlled manual intervention |
| Snapshot audit | Disposition keeps a snapshot of the adjudication basis and affected list, attributable down to why banned and whom banned | The goal of audit is not keeping logs, but being able to attribute responsibility |
| Appeal closed loop | Appeal evidence packs generated automatically: adjudication basis + behavior replay + restoration path | Why risk control cannot stop at detection |
| Evidence aggregation | The support aggregation query interface pulls all six data classes in one call, front line never touches the production database | What makes support hard is cross-system evidence collection |

### Three lessons from the case

1. **A hit is not the end of governance** — with the appeal link missing from the five-link closed loop, a **42.5**% false-positive rate could only be discovered through public sentiment rather than by the system itself.
2. **Strong writes must go through a controlled side channel** — automatic banning that bypasses GM layering turns "controlled manual intervention" into "unreviewed automatic strong writes".
3. **Evidence packs are the cure for disputes** — whether the team can produce evidence, carry out disposition and restore order within an acceptable time is the real mark of governance maturity.

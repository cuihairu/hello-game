# English Terms and Etymology

## Category

Glossary tree node. Quick-reference positioning: it collects the English terms and genre abbreviations that recur throughout the book, each entry giving the original English term, the Chinese translation, a one-line sense, source/etymology, and the landing page on site; it complements the nine "term conventions" chapters — those answer the Chinese architecture words (who is responsible, where state lives, who catches failure), while this page answers what the English technical words are and where they come from.

## Definition

English terms in game development come in three kinds: those with a traceable source (papers, official documentation, GDC talks, source code), industry-standard engineering words, and community slang. When the three are mixed together, the easiest way to get led astray is "etymology marketing" — passing an industry-standard word off as coming from a certain paper, or passing a community-practice word off as an academic concept. Every entry on this page is collected under the same four-piece format: the original English term and Chinese translation; a one-line sense (only the judgment sentence; the mechanism is left to the landing page on site); source/etymology (labeled honestly by verifiability); and the landing page on site (cross-links to the page that expands the mechanism).

## Problem

The most common defect in terminology articles is inventing etymologies: writing "sounds like I heard it somewhere" as "comes from such-and-such paper," and once a reader cites it the error propagates. This page's rule is rather leave it blank than make it up — where a source can be found, give the source (paper/official documentation/source code/GDC talk); industry-standard engineering words get labeled "standard"; where a first citation genuinely cannot be found, label it "source untraced." The sources record only facts that are "publicly verifiable and already checked on site": the rating entries are consistent with the [PVP Matchmaking](/system/pvp-matchmaking) page's sense, and the open-source-component positioning is consistent with the commit anchoring of the [Open-Source Server Map](/server/map).

## Scope and judgment criteria

The first version extracted terms from the sync, networking, runtime, system, numerical, BI, history and game-library pages plus the [Genre and Component Coverage Map](/system/genre-coverage), grouped into four themes: networking and sync, engine and runtime, gameplay and genres, numerical and economy — 74 entries in total (including 9 open-source components; 17 entries added in the 2026-10-09 batch alongside the gameplay-system expansion). Entries do not chase a single industry standard (the sense of those nine term chapters applies here too); only two things are guaranteed: the Chinese translations match the site's body text, and the sources match the source-code facts already checked on site. The 2026-10-14 batch added clickable links to the verifiable sources of 41 entries (RFCs, Valve and Epic official documentation, GDC talks, a Microsoft Research paper, open-source repositories and Wikipedia), plus repository links for 9 open-source components; every link was verified reachable by test before being written in. Later additions roll in with new pages.

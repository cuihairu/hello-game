# The Difference Between the Main Line and the Appendices

The main line and the appendices carry materials of different natures: the main line is a chain of argument, with each chapter supplying premises to the ones before and after it; the appendices are a set of comparison tables and topical pieces that provide a cross-cutting view.

## Why the Main Line Is Organized as a Chain of Argument

There are real dependencies between main-line chapters: the problem model gives cost judgments, network access and sync and combat give the constraints of the main chain, the execution model and service splitting take up those constraints, and data, version, and operations then expand outward. Remove an intermediate link and the later conclusions are left as bare conclusions that will judge wrongly the moment you switch projects. So the chapter order of the main line is an order of argument, not exactly an order of development workflow; reordering the reading according to an engineering schedule often breaks this chain.

## Why the Appendices Sit Outside the Main Line

Appendix content shares two traits. First, it is cross-cutting: the same kind of topic is scattered across many chapters by type, by problem domain, and by tool, and compressing it into a comparison actually saves effort. Second, it depends more heavily on concrete form: content like config-table pipelines, load-test steps, and engine checklists changes fast with tools and stages, and putting it in the main line would make the chain of argument carry a burden of frequent revision. Letting the appendices carry concrete form while the main line holds the abstract judgment puts each in its place.

## A Test for Deciding Where Content Belongs

When unsure which side a piece of content belongs to, the common practice is to ask: remove it, does the main line's chain of argument break? If it breaks, the piece carries an argumentative duty and belongs to the main line; if it doesn't break, it is a cross-cutting supplement and belongs to the appendices. This standard can also be used to organize a team's internal documentation.

## Differences in How the Main Line and Appendices Are Organized

The main line unfolds reasoning and cost, depending on earlier and later chapters for background; the appendices compress things into comparisons and checklists, which makes them easier to locate and verify. They differ in style of writing, taking up the process of judgment and operational questions respectively.

## What Each Group of Appendix Content Serves

- Game-type topics: provide genre intuition, answering where products of this type usually compress their complexity.
- Per-problem-domain summaries: cross-cutting comparison, compressing content across many chapters into one verifiable table.
- Config pipeline: a collaboration workflow, answering how the whole chain from editing config to it taking effect is governed.
- Capacity scaling: the operational surface of launch preparation and long-term governance — load testing, planning, and scaling up and down.
- Engines, languages, and tools: background material for technology selection, placing the tool ecosystem back inside business and team constraints.

## Seeing the Division on the Examples of Config and Capacity

- For config: the main line discusses the boundary between config-driven and script-driven in the scripts and hot update chapter, answering "what changes should not go through a full release"; the config pipeline group in the appendices discusses how tables flow, how they are validated, and how people collaborate, answering "how does config safely reach production."
- For capacity: the main line discusses how to judge that a system is nearing its limit and where jitter comes from in the observability and stability chapter; the capacity scaling group in the appendices discusses the concrete organization of load testing, capacity planning, and scaling.

A similar relationship also appears between the problem model and the game-type topics: the former gives abstract dimensions, the latter looks back at those dimensions per genre to see where each is compressed.

## Why a Piece of Content Goes Here Rather Than There

The standard is usually whether it answers a judgment question or an operational question. What kinds of change config-driven should carry — that is a judgment question, so it goes in the main line; how tables are validated and how they flow — that is an operational question, so it goes in the appendices. How to judge that a system is nearing its limit goes in the main line; how to organize load testing goes in the appendices. This standard can also be used to organize a team's internal documentation.

## When to Step Away from the Main Line Into the Appendices

Three moments are most worth it: when a design review needs cross-comparison, take the appendices to check common landing points; when inventorying an older project, use the appendices as a checklist to go through item by item; when supplementing intuition for a specific genre, read the type topics to see where similar products compress their costs. The appendices are for cross-checking and cannot replace the process of judgment in the main line.

## What the Appendices Cannot Replace

What the appendices give are common landing points, not the process of judgment. For the same line — "products of this type have their cost in numerical design and economy" — someone who knows why can judge whether their own project belongs to that type, while someone who does not can only copy the conclusion. This is also why the appendices are repeatedly told to "read the main line first": without the main line, a comparison table is just a table.

## Looking Again at the Division Between the Problem Model and the Type Topics

The problem model gives abstract dimensions: the range of state sharing, the way time advances, asset complexity, and operational intensity. The type topics look back per genre: for products of the same type, what gameplay weights down each of those dimensions. The former records a method of judgment; the latter provides reference samples. Reading only the latter leaves you without a handle on projects of mixed form; reading only the former removes a layer of shared language in communicating with designers and publishers.

## How the Main Line and Appendices Intersect Across Project Stages

After a project enters launch preparation and long-term operations, more operational questions such as load testing, migration, and config collaboration appear. At that point the appendices can be used to check operational questions like load testing, migration, and config collaboration. The engines, languages, and tools group is the same — it is background material for selection, not a leaderboard.

## The Update Cadence of the Two Sides Differs Too

The main line discusses judgments that hold over the long term, so it updates slowly; the tools, genres, and process forms in the appendices update quickly. This also affects citation: when writing internal proposals, citing the main line's judgments is usually more durable than citing the appendices' concrete forms, and the appendices are better suited as cross-check material for current operations.

When similar-to-appendix content is encountered while reading the main line straight through, there is no need to unfold it on the spot; mark it and look it up at the corresponding stage, which is usually less effort than jumping out of the argument midway.

## Walking Through the Division with a Real Problem

Suppose the problem is "an event config was released, but some players received old values." The main-line entry is the config-driven boundary in the scripts and hot update chapter and the compatibility window in the version release chapter, which answer why a mismatched version combination occurs; the appendix entry is the validation and distribution flow in the config pipeline group, which answers which link in this distribution missed validation. The main line gives the judgment framework, the appendices give the checkpoints; using both together means the problem is fully covered.

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating the appendices as a shortcut, drawing conclusions directly from comparison tables and skipping the main line's chain of judgment.
- Using the main line's abstract conclusions directly as an operations manual, ignoring the differences in concrete form in the appendices.
- Assuming the appendices are low-priority content and not reading them at all, so the config or tool parts rely on hearsay for decisions over the long run.
- Mixing the main line and appendices together, which easily conflates the reasoning background with an operational checklist.
